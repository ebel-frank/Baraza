## Practice Note 1: Jurisdiction of Customary Courts
Customary Courts commonly handle matters of marriage, inheritance, and land held under
customary tenure between persons subject to the same or similar customary law. They generally
do not have jurisdiction over criminal matters, land already converted to statutory title, or
disputes where one party is not subject to customary law.

## Practice Note 2: Inheritance Under Customary Law
Customary law inheritance disputes often turn on the personal law of the deceased's community
(e.g., patrilineal or matrilineal succession rules), which can differ significantly by
ethnicity and region. Community mediators are well placed to make an initial record of the
family's account, but a formal customary court ruling is typically needed once multiple
branches of a family actively dispute the distribution.

## Practice Note 3: Repeat and Escalating Disputes
Customary courts and community elders have both noted that disputes which have already been
"settled" informally more than once, without resolving the underlying grievance, are a strong
signal that the matter needs a different (often more formal) forum, since continued informal
settlement can allow tension to build rather than resolve.

## Practice Note 4: Domestic and Gender-Based Violence
Where a family or marital dispute involves allegations of physical violence, customary
mediation should not proceed as if it were a purely civil disagreement. Such cases should be
directed toward the Police and, where relevant, a Family Support Unit, in parallel with any
customary process.
