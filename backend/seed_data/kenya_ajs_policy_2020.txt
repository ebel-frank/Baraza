## Section 2.1: Definition and Scope of AJS
Alternative Justice Systems (AJS) refers to the traditional, informal, and community-based
mechanisms used to resolve disputes outside the formal court system, including councils of
elders, religious and cultural forums, and community mediation. AJS is recognized as
complementary to the formal justice system, not a replacement for it, particularly where
constitutional rights or criminal conduct are engaged.

## Section 3.4: Limits of AJS Jurisdiction
AJS mechanisms should not be used to resolve matters involving gross human rights violations,
sexual and gender-based violence, defilement, or other criminal offences. Such matters must be
referred to the formal justice system, including the Police and the Judiciary, regardless of
whether the parties wish to settle informally.

## Section 4.2: Land and Succession Disputes
AJS forums may mediate boundary and land-use disagreements between neighbors where no
registered title is in dispute. Where a dispute concerns a registered title deed, or where
fraud in a land transaction is alleged, the matter should be referred to the Land Registrar
or the Environment and Land Court.

## Section 5.1: Repeat Disputes
Where a dispute has already been the subject of a prior mediation between the same parties
and has recurred, AJS practitioners are encouraged to consider referral to a higher or formal
forum, since repeated informal settlement may indicate an unresolved underlying issue.

## Section 6.3: Safeguards for Vulnerable Parties
Special care must be taken where a party is a minor, an elderly person, or otherwise
vulnerable. AJS practitioners should not finalize a settlement affecting a child's welfare
without involving the relevant Children's Officer.
