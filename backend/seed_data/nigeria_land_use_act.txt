## Section 1: Vesting of Land in the Governor
All land in a State is vested in the Governor, who holds it in trust for the benefit of all
Nigerians. Individuals and communities may hold land under a Right of Occupancy rather than
outright freehold ownership.

## Section 5: Statutory Right of Occupancy
The Governor may grant a Statutory Right of Occupancy for land in urban areas. Disputes over
the validity or boundaries of a Right of Occupancy that has already been formally granted
should be referred to the relevant Land Registry or the courts, rather than settled informally.

## Section 6: Customary Right of Occupancy
Local Government authorities may grant a Customary Right of Occupancy for land in non-urban
areas. Disputes between neighboring holders of customary rights of occupancy, including farm
boundary disagreements, are commonly handled through community and customary mediation before
escalating to formal forums.

## Section 34: Existing Customary Rights on Undeveloped Land
A person who was in lawful occupation of land under customary law immediately before the Act
took effect is deemed to hold a Right of Occupancy over the developed portion of that land.
Disputes about who was in lawful occupation at that time, especially where inheritance is also
contested, often require documentary or elder testimony and are well suited to community
mediation as a first step.
